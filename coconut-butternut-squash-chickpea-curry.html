Coconut Butternut Squash & Chickpea Curry

Serves 6 generous portions (3 meals for 2 people) · Difficulty: Easy

Prep: 20 minutes · Cook: 30–35 minutes · Total: 50–55 minutes · Source: User-provided recipe

A cozy Thai-inspired autumn curry featuring tender butternut squash, hearty chickpeas, spinach, and a fragrant coconut sauce. Served over jasmine rice with fresh lime.

Ingredients

Curry

  • 1 large butternut squash (about 3 lb whole, yielding roughly 6–7 cups cubed)
  • 3 cans (15 oz each) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 2 cans (13.5 oz each) full-fat coconut milk
  • 5–6 cups fresh baby spinach
  • 1 large yellow onion, diced
  • 5 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1½ tbsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 4 tbsp Thai Kitchen red curry paste (use 2½–3 tbsp if using a stronger brand like Mae Ploy)
  • 1½ tbsp neutral cooking oil
  • ½ cup vegetable broth, plus up to ¼ cup more as needed
  • 1½ tbsp soy sauce
  • 1½ tsp brown sugar (optional, add only if needed)
  • 1–2 limes
  • Salt, to taste

Rice & Garnishes

  • 2¼ cups uncooked jasmine rice
  • Fresh cilantro or scallions (optional)
  • Roasted cashews or peanuts (optional)

Directions

  1. Prepare ingredients and rice (15–20 min). Peel, seed, and cut the butternut squash into approximately ¾-inch cubes. Dice the onion, mince the garlic, and grate the ginger. Rinse the jasmine rice and cook according to package instructions, timing it to finish alongside the curry.
  2. Build the curry base (7–8 min). Heat the oil in a large Dutch oven (at least 5–6 quarts) over medium heat. Add the diced onion and sauté for 5–6 minutes until softened and beginning to turn golden. Add the garlic, ginger, and red curry paste. Stir continuously for 1–2 minutes until fragrant, taking care not to burn the paste.
  3. Simmer the curry (20–25 min). Add the cubed butternut squash, drained chickpeas, coconut milk, ½ cup vegetable broth, and soy sauce. Stir thoroughly, scraping the bottom of the pot to incorporate the curry paste. Bring to a gentle simmer, then reduce the heat to medium-low. Cover loosely and simmer for 20–25 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the squash is fork-tender but still holds its shape. If too thick, add the remaining ¼ cup broth a little at a time. If too thin, remove the lid and simmer uncovered for 5–10 minutes. For a naturally thicker sauce, gently mash a few pieces of squash into the curry.
  4. Add spinach (1–2 min). Stir in the fresh spinach in batches, allowing each batch to wilt before adding more. Taste the curry and adjust seasoning as needed. If additional curry paste is desired, briefly fry it in a little oil in a separate pan before stirring it into the curry.
  5. Finish and serve (2 min). Remove the pot from heat. Stir in 1½ tbsp fresh lime juice. Taste and adjust: more lime juice for brightness and acidity, more soy sauce or salt for savoriness, or a small amount of brown sugar if the sauce tastes overly sharp or spicy. Serve over jasmine rice and garnish with cilantro, scallions, or toasted nuts.

Portioning & Meal Prep

Divide the finished curry into 6 equal portions. The rice should yield approximately 6–7 cups cooked, providing roughly 1 cup per serving.

Storage

  • Refrigerate promptly in airtight containers.
  • Keep rice separate from the curry when practical.
  • Refrigerate for up to 3–4 days.
  • Freeze portions that won't be eaten within 4 days.
  • Reheat thoroughly, adding a splash of water to loosen the sauce.

Cooking Notes

  • Curry paste: Thai Kitchen is relatively mild, so 4 tbsp is a reasonable starting point. Stronger brands like Mae Ploy may only need 2½–3 tbsp. Check ingredients for shrimp paste if cooking vegetarian.
  • Coconut milk: Use full-fat for a rich, creamy sauce. Two cans provide plenty of liquid, so start with only ½ cup broth.
  • Chickpeas: Three cans make this particularly hearty and filling, ideal for complete meals rather than a side dish.
  • Squash: Keep pieces uniformly sized. Slightly firm squash holds up better when reheated.
  • Flavor balance: Taste before adding sugar. The squash and coconut milk already contribute natural sweetness.
  • Lime: Always add at the end to preserve its fresh, bright flavor.
  • Pot size: A 6-quart Dutch oven is recommended for this larger batch.
