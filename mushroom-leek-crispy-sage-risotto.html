Mushroom, Leek & Crispy Sage Risotto

Serves 2 generous portions · Time: 40 minutes · Source: User-provided recipe

Creamy mushroom and leek risotto with Parmesan, lemon juice, and crispy sage.

Ingredients

  • 180 g (about 1 cup) Arborio or Carnaroli rice
  • 10–12 oz (285–340 g) mushrooms, sliced or torn
  • 1 medium leek, white and light-green parts only, thinly sliced and washed well
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 10–12 fresh sage leaves
  • ½ cup dry white wine
  • 3 cups (710 ml) chicken or vegetable stock
  • 3 tbsp unsalted butter, divided
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • ½ cup (45–50 g) finely grated Parmesan
  • 1–2 tsp fresh lemon juice
  • Kosher salt
  • Freshly ground black pepper
  • Optional: ½ oz (10–15 g) dried porcini mushrooms

Directions

  1. Heat the stock. Keep the stock hot over low heat while you cook. If using dried porcini, soak them in about ½ cup hot stock for 15–20 minutes. Chop the porcini and return the strained soaking liquid to the stock.
  2. Brown the mushrooms. Heat the olive oil and 1 tbsp butter in a wide pan over medium-high heat. Add the mushrooms in a single layer, working in batches if necessary. Cook for 5–7 minutes until deeply browned. Season with salt and pepper. Reserve about ⅓ of the mushrooms for topping.
  3. Crisp the sage. Add ½ tbsp butter to the mushroom pan. Fry the sage leaves for 30–60 seconds, until crisp. Transfer to a paper towel and lightly salt.
  4. Cook the leek. In a wide saucepan or deep skillet, melt ½ tbsp butter over medium-low heat. Add the leek and a pinch of salt. Cook for 5–7 minutes until very soft but not browned. Add the garlic and cook for another 30–60 seconds.
  5. Toast the rice. Add the rice and cook for 2–3 minutes, stirring frequently, until coated in butter and slightly translucent around the edges.
  6. Add the wine. Pour in the wine and cook for 1–2 minutes, stirring, until almost completely absorbed.
  7. Cook the risotto. Add about ½ cup hot stock and keep the rice at a gentle simmer. Stir regularly. Once the liquid is mostly absorbed, add another ½ cup. Continue adding stock gradually. After about 10 minutes, stir in the non-reserved mushrooms. Continue cooking until the rice is creamy and tender but still slightly firm in the center, about 18–22 minutes total. You may not need every drop of stock.
  8. Finish off the heat. The risotto should still be fairly loose when finished. Remove from the heat and vigorously stir in 1 tbsp cold butter, Parmesan, 1–2 tsp lemon juice, and freshly ground black pepper. Taste and adjust salt.
  9. Serve immediately. Divide between two bowls and top with the reserved browned mushrooms, crispy sage, extra Parmesan and black pepper.
Key tips:
  • Don't crowd the mushrooms. Browning them properly gives the risotto much more flavor.
  • Keep the stock hot. Cold stock slows the cooking each time you add it.
  • Don't wait until the risotto looks thick enough. Take it off the heat while it's slightly looser than you want; it thickens rapidly.
  • The finished risotto should slowly spread on the plate, not sit in a stiff mound.
  • If it gets too thick, stir in a few tablespoons of hot stock before serving.
