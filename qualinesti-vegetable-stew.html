Qualinesti Vegetable Stew

Serves 4 · Source: Heroes' Feast cookbook

A baked vegetable casserole with zucchini, yellow squash, eggplant, and tomatoes over a basil-tomato sauce, finished with garlic-thyme oil.

Ingredients

  • 1/3 cup extra-virgin olive oil
  • 5 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 2 teaspoons minced fresh thyme
  • 1 large yellow onion, finely chopped
  • Kosher salt
  • 1 (15-ounce) can tomato puree
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • Freshly ground black pepper
  • 1/4 cup chopped fresh basil
  • 1 zucchini, sliced 1/4 inch thick
  • 1 yellow squash, sliced 1/4 inch thick
  • 1 Japanese or Chinese eggplant, sliced 1/4 inch thick
  • 4 plum tomatoes, sliced 1/4 inch thick

Directions

  1. Preheat the oven to 375°F with a rack in the middle position.
  2. In a 12-inch ovenproof skillet over medium heat, combine 1/4 cup of the olive oil, half of the garlic, and the thyme. Stir until fragrant and the garlic begins to sizzle, 1 to 2 minutes. Transfer the mixture to a small bowl.
  3. Warm the remaining olive oil in the skillet over medium heat. Add the onion and 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt, then cook until softened, about 4 minutes. Add the remaining garlic and cook until fragrant, about 1 minute.
  4. Stir in the tomato puree, sugar, and black pepper to taste. Simmer, stirring occasionally, until slightly thickened, about 7 minutes. Add more salt if needed, remove from the heat, and let cool slightly.
  5. Mix 2 tablespoons of the basil into the sauce and spread it evenly across the skillet.
  6. Arrange the zucchini, yellow squash, eggplant, and tomato slices in alternating, overlapping concentric circles, packing them tightly into one layer.
  7. Drizzle the reserved garlic-thyme oil over the vegetables. Season with about 3/4 teaspoon kosher salt and 3/4 teaspoon black pepper.
  8. Cover and bake until the vegetables begin to soften, about 30 minutes. Uncover and bake until very tender and bubbling around the edges, about 20 minutes more.
  9. Rest for 10 minutes, sprinkle with the remaining 2 tablespoons basil, and serve.
Cook's note: Japanese and Chinese eggplants are long and slender. When using a globe eggplant, select a narrow one and cut slices close in size to the squash and tomatoes.
